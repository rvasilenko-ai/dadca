# Slide 18 — Selecting what deserves investigation

**Timing:** 71–75 minutes  
**Module:** LLMs as analysts

## On-slide content

Prioritization signals:

- Detection uncertainty
- Novelty
- Prevalence
- Potential impact
- Similarity to an active campaign
- Expected information gain
- Analyst and compute capacity

> Which 10 artifacts deserve 30 minutes each?

## Speaker notes

Frame selective reverse engineering as a research problem spanning ranking, active learning, decision theory, resource allocation, and security operations.

A rare artifact is not automatically important; a common artifact is not automatically understood. Prioritization should account for both epistemic value and operational risk.

This slide also bridges to Lecture 2, where the same kind of LLM receives tools, a selected objective, and a constrained environment on the attacker's side.
