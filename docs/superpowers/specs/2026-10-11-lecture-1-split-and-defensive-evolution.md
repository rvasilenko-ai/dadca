# Lecture 1 Split and Defensive Evolution — Design

**Date:** 2026-10-11  
**Scope:** Move the machine-learning detector material out of Lecture 1 into a new Lecture 3, and add an explicit account of defensive product evolution to Lecture 1.  
**Supersedes:** the Lecture 1 slide allocation in `2026-08-05-lecture-rebalance-design.md` (its Lecture 2 content is unaffected; its Project Ire slides now belong to Lecture 3).

## Problems addressed

1. **Density.** Lecture 1 carried 33 slides in 90 minutes, with five technical ML concepts (PE structure, EMBER/SOREL, MinHash/LSH, MalConv, BERT over disassembly) at about three minutes each, and two exercises at four and two minutes. The audience was also changed from Master's/PhD to undergraduate/Master's, which makes the density worse.
2. **Missing argument.** The lecture explained where evidence disappears in the pipeline but never stated the historical consequence: each defensive product class is built around one unit of evidence, and as malware moved from file to capability, and the web moved to HTTPS, specific product classes lost effectiveness. Sandboxes and network IDS/IPS are the two clearest cases.
3. **Internal tension.** The lecture argued for twenty minutes that malware is not a file, then spent twenty minutes on classifiers whose only input is a file. In a dedicated ML lecture this becomes a deliberate teaching point instead of an unexamined contradiction.

## Decisions

### Series

Three standalone lectures. Lecture 1 for undergraduate and Master's students; Lectures 2 and 3 for Master's and PhD. Directory `lecture-2/` keeps its name and content; the new lecture is `lecture-3/`.

## Claims to keep qualified on slides

- Sandboxes are "commodity and narrow", not "useless". They retain roles against commodity droppers and malicious documents at machine scale and as an analyst tool at investigation scale.
- IDS/IPS lost north-south payload inspection without decryption. East-west plaintext protocols, DNS without encryption, scanning, beacon timing, and TLS fingerprints remain observable.
- HTTPS adoption figures are rounded and must be checked against the live Google Transparency Report and Firefox telemetry charts before delivery.
- The remote-management and BYOVD comparison (July plan constraint) lives in the ATT&CK module (slide 17) as the example of capabilities delivered through benign files, with ATT&CK identifiers and a scoring on the four axes. The four-axis model no longer has a slide of its own: it was found to compete with ATT&CK as a second abstraction at the same point in the narrative, so the axes are named on slide 4 with the five opening questions, the per-period axis lives in the recap notes, and the scoring on slide 17 exercises them.
- The motive track is named "Motive and money", not "Where is the money", so that espionage and prepositioning fit without pretending they are financial. The agent period's business model is stated as unobserved.
- Period-slide cells describe the dominant tradecraft of a period and what it removed from the previous defense's view, not a claim that any named example was the first of its kind.
